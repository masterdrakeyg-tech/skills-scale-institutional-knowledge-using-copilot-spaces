# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This documentation describes how OctoAcme initiates, plans, executes, releases, and improves cross-functional projects. It provides a shared source of truth for roles, workflows, communication, quality expectations, and project artifacts.

## Project management overview

OctoAcme projects begin with initiation to confirm the business need, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, and initial risks. Approved work moves into planning, where teams hold a kickoff, prioritize and estimate the backlog, define acceptance criteria and a Definition of Done, identify dependencies and risks, and establish the release plan. Execution follows an iterative, transparent cadence using a project board, daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, reviews, and tracked metrics.

Accountability is shared across clear personas: Project Managers coordinate delivery, schedules, risks, dependencies, and communications; Product Managers define outcomes, prioritize the roadmap and backlog, and measure success; Developers implement, test, review, and maintain the solution; and QA/testing validates quality and acceptance criteria. Stakeholders provide requirements, feedback, decisions, and approvals. Recurring project-manager and product-manager alignment, delivery-team standups, milestone updates, and documented status reports keep these roles aligned.

Quality is built into delivery through small pull requests with linked issues and acceptance criteria, automated unit, integration, and security checks, CI validation, code review, smoke tests, and manual feature acceptance where needed. Risks are maintained in a register and escalated through team, project, product, sponsor, or incident-specific channels as appropriate. Releases require passing checks, staging verification, rollback or mitigation plans, post-deployment verification, and stakeholder communication.

After sprints, releases, incidents, or important milestones, teams hold retrospectives to capture what went well and what should improve. Prioritized action items receive owners and due dates, are tracked alongside project work, and are reviewed for impact as part of OctoAcme’s continuous improvement culture.
